# Your freelance tax/bookkeeping system (first year, US)

## Do this

**1. Open a separate business bank account (this week).**
Any free business checking account works (Novo, Found, or your existing bank's business account). Every client payment goes in here, every business expense comes out of here. Don't mix personal and business money — it's the #1 thing that turns tax season into a nightmare.

**2. Set aside 25-30% of every payment for taxes, immediately.**
Open a separate high-yield savings account labeled "taxes." The moment you get paid, transfer 25-30% of that payment into it. Don't touch it. This covers federal income tax + self-employment tax (Social Security/Medicare — as a freelancer you pay both the employee and employer share, ~15.3% on top of income tax).

**3. Pay quarterly estimated taxes.**
The IRS wants you to pay as you go, not one lump sum in April. Use IRS Form 1040-ES.
- Due dates (2026): Jan 15, Apr 15, Jun 15, Sep 15
- Pay online at irs.gov/payments (Direct Pay, no account needed) — takes 5 minutes
- Send roughly 25-30% of that quarter's income (the money already sitting in your tax savings account)
- Also check your state — most states with income tax want quarterly payments too (search "[your state] estimated tax payment")

**4. Track income and expenses with one tool: Wave (free) or a simple spreadsheet.**
Wave (waveapps.com) is free, built for freelancers, and connects to your bank account to auto-import transactions. Set it up once, connect your business bank account, and spend 10 minutes a month tagging transactions as business expenses.
If you'd rather not add a tool: a simple spreadsheet with columns (Date, Client, Amount, Category) for income, and (Date, Vendor, Amount, Category) for expenses, works fine in year one.

**5. Save every receipt for business expenses.**
Software subscriptions (Figma, Adobe), a portion of your phone/internet bill, a home office (if you have a dedicated space), equipment, courses. Photograph receipts and drop them in a "Tax Year 2026" folder in Google Drive/Dropbox as you go — not in April.

**6. Get invoices right from day one.**
Every invoice needs: your name/business name, client name, date, description of work, amount, payment terms. Free invoicing is built into Wave. This isn't optional — it's your paper trail if a client disputes payment or the IRS asks questions.

**7. In January 2027, expect 1099-NEC forms.**
Any client who paid you $600+ in 2026 should send you a 1099-NEC by Jan 31, 2027. You don't need to chase this — your own records (the spreadsheet/Wave) are what matters for your tax return, not waiting on their forms.

**8. File taxes with a real person or TurboTax Self-Employed, not the free version.**
Your first year, pay for either a CPA/EA (search "enrolled agent near me," ~$200-500) or TurboTax Self-Employed / FreeTaxUSA (self-employed tier, ~$0-120). A human preparer for year one is worth it — they'll catch deductions you don't know exist and you can reuse their setup yourself next year.

## Careful

- **Skipping quarterly payments costs real money.** The IRS charges an underpayment penalty (interest, currently ~8%/year) if you owe more than $1,000 at filing time without having paid quarterly. This is the most common "got screwed" moment for new freelancers — avoid it by actually sending the 1040-ES payments above.
- **Spending the "tax" money is the second most common disaster.** Treat that savings account as if it isn't yours. If you're tempted, move it to a bank you don't have a debit card for.
- **Don't guess on deductions.** Home office, mileage, and equipment deductions have specific rules (e.g., home office must be used *exclusively* for work). Claiming something you don't qualify for is an audit risk. This is exactly what a CPA/EA for year one solves — ask them, don't guess.

## Check it worked

At the end of any month, open Wave (or your spreadsheet): every client payment received that month should appear as income, every business purchase should be tagged, and your "tax" savings account balance should equal roughly 25-30% of that month's income. If a payment or expense is missing, you've found a gap before it becomes a April surprise.

## Decided for you

- Chose Wave over QuickBooks because it's free and sufficient for a solo freelancer's first year — QuickBooks earns its cost once you have employees or much higher volume.
- Chose a separate savings account over trying to "calculate as you go" because a visible, untouchable balance is the only version of this that actually survives contact with real life.
- Chose 25-30% as the set-aside rate — self-employment tax alone is 15.3%, plus federal income tax (10-22% bracket for most new freelancers), plus state tax if applicable. This is a safe estimate to confirm with a preparer once you know your actual income level; it's fine to end up refunding yourself some of it later.
- Chose IRS Direct Pay over mailing checks because it's free, instant, and gives you a confirmation number as proof of payment.

## Only you can decide

- **Which state you're in** — determines whether you owe state estimated taxes too, and the deadline/portal differs by state. Assumed you'll check this yourself via "[your state] estimated tax."
- **Whether you have a dedicated home office space** — changes whether the home office deduction applies to you. Assumed no for now (safer default); ask your preparer.
- **Your total expected 2026 income** — determines whether 25% or 30% set-aside is closer to right. Assumed 25-30% as a safe range; a CPA can tighten this once you tell them your numbers.
