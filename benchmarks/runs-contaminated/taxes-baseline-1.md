**Do this**

1. **Open a dedicated business checking account** (any free one — Chase, Novo, Bluevine, whatever your current bank offers). Move every client payment into it, pay every business expense from it. Don't touch it for personal stuff. This one habit prevents 90% of bookkeeping pain.
2. **Get a bookkeeping app**: Wave (free) or QuickBooks Self-Employed (~$15/mo, built for freelancers, auto-calculates quarterly taxes). For your first year with modest income, start with Wave — free, connects to your bank, tracks income/expenses.
3. **Every time you get paid**, log it in the app (or let bank sync do it) and tag it "income."
4. **Every time you buy something for work** (software, a monitor, Adobe subscription, a coworking desk, portion of internet bill), save the receipt (photo is fine — Wave lets you snap and attach) and tag it "business expense."
5. **Set aside 25–30% of every payment** the moment it lands, into a separate high-yield savings account labeled "taxes." This is the single most important habit. Freelancers get blindsided because no one withholds taxes for you — you owe both income tax and self-employment tax (~15.3% extra for Social Security/Medicare on top of regular income tax).
6. **Pay quarterly estimated taxes** to the IRS. Deadlines this year/next: Jan 15, Apr 15, Jun 15, Sep 15. Use IRS Direct Pay (irs.gov/payments, free, no account needed) — pick "estimated tax" as the reason. If you underpay by a lot, the IRS charges a small penalty, so paying roughly on time matters more than paying exactly right.
7. **Ask every client for a signed contract or at least a written agreement** before starting work, and send invoices (Wave has free invoicing built in). If any single client pays you $600+ in the year, they're supposed to send you a 1099 — keep your own records regardless, since you're responsible for reporting all income even without one.
8. **In January 2027**, once you have a full year of data in Wave, either do your own return with a tool like TurboTax Self-Employed/H&R Block (fine if things stayed simple) or hand your Wave export to a CPA (better if your income was substantial or things got messy — budget $300–600 for a freelancer return).

**Careful**

- **Commingling funds** (using one account for both personal and business) is the #1 thing that turns "simple" into "nightmare" later — always pay from the dedicated account.
- **Not saving for taxes as you go** is the #1 way freelancers "get screwed" — a $20k year can mean $3,000–5,000 owed at tax time, and if it's not set aside, you're borrowing to pay it.
- **Missing quarterly deadlines entirely** (not just paying the wrong amount) triggers IRS underpayment penalties — even a rough estimate paid on time beats a perfect number paid late.
- **Losing receipts** — without them, deductions are deductions the IRS can disallow if you're ever asked to prove them. Snap a photo the moment you buy something; don't rely on memory later.

**Check it worked**

At the end of any month, open Wave: total income minus total expenses should roughly match what's sitting in your business checking account (minus what you moved to tax savings). If those numbers don't line up, something wasn't logged — go find it before it piles up.

**Decided for you**

- Chose Wave over QuickBooks because it's free and sufficient for year one; upgrade only if your income or expense volume gets complex.
- Chose a 25–30% tax set-aside over a precise calculation because in year one you don't have last year's numbers to base "safe harbor" estimates on — over-setting-aside is safer than under.
- Chose quarterly IRS Direct Pay over mailing checks because it's free, immediate, and leaves a digital receipt.
- Chose "separate bank account" as the foundational step over any app, because no software fixes commingled money.

**Only you can decide**

- What state you're in (state income tax rules and quarterly deadlines vary — assumed you'll check your state's revenue department site; some states like TX/FL/WA have no income tax).
- Roughly how much you expect to earn this year (assumed modest/part-time freelance income — if you're already clearing $80k+, get a CPA now instead of waiting until January, since an LLC/S-corp election could save you real money).
- Whether you want to handle April's return yourself or pay a CPA (assumed self-file via TurboTax is fine for a first simple year; revisit once income grows).
