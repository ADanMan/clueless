Totally doable — freelance taxes/bookkeeping are annoying but the *system* is simple once it's set up. Here's a "set it and forget it" setup for a US freelance designer, first year.

## 1. Separate your money (do this first, today)

- Open a **separate business checking account** (any free business or even a second personal checking account works for year one). All client payments go in here. All business expenses come out of here. Never mix with personal spending.
- Get a **separate debit or credit card** tied to that account for business purchases (software, subscriptions, equipment).

This one habit does 80% of the bookkeeping work for you automatically, because now your bank statement *is* your ledger.

## 2. Track income and expenses (15 minutes, done once)

- Sign up for **Bookkeeping/accounting software** — easiest for a solo freelancer: **Wave** (free) or **QuickBooks Self-Employed** (~$15/mo, built specifically for freelancers and estimates taxes for you).
- Connect it to your business bank account and card. It will auto-import and categorize transactions. You just glance at it monthly and approve categories.
- Send **invoices through the same tool** (Wave has free invoicing) so income is tracked automatically too.

## 3. Save for taxes as you go (the #1 thing that screws freelancers)

Nobody withholds taxes for you now, so you must do it yourself or you'll owe a scary lump sum in April.

- **Every time you get paid, immediately move 25–30% of it into a separate "taxes" savings account.** Treat it as not your money.
- Why 25-30%: this covers federal income tax + **self-employment tax** (15.3% for Social Security/Medicare, which is the part employees don't see because employers normally split it with them).
- If your state has income tax, bump to 28-32%.

## 4. Pay quarterly estimated taxes

The IRS wants its cut 4x a year, not just in April, once you owe more than ~$1,000 for the year (almost certain in your first year if you're earning anything real).

- Due dates (2026): **Apr 15, Jun 15, Sep 15, Jan 15** (of the following year).
- Use **IRS Direct Pay** (irs.gov/payments) — free, takes 5 minutes, no account needed.
- How much to pay: your bookkeeping software (QuickBooks Self-Employed especially) will estimate this for you. Rough manual method: take your profit so far this quarter × ~25-30%, pay that.
- Missing these has a small penalty, but it's minor — don't stress, just try to pay something close each quarter.

## 5. Track deductible expenses (lowers what you owe)

As a designer, these are almost certainly deductible — keep receipts (your card + Wave/QuickBooks auto-tracks most of this):
- Software subscriptions (Adobe, Figma, fonts, etc.)
- Computer/monitor/tablet (equipment)
- A portion of home internet if you work from home
- Home office (a % of rent/utilities if you have a dedicated space — the "simplified method" is $5/sq ft, up to 300 sq ft, easy and safe)
- Business portion of phone bill
- Professional development (courses, books)
- Portion of health insurance premiums if self-employed (can be a big deduction)

Don't overthink this — just dump receipts into the software and let it categorize; review once a month for 10 minutes.

## 6. At year-end (filing)

- You'll get **1099-NEC forms** from any client who paid you $600+ (they're required to send by Jan 31). You don't need to wait for these to know your numbers — your bookkeeping software has the totals.
- File **Schedule C** (business profit/loss) and **Schedule SE** (self-employment tax) along with your regular 1040.
- First year, strongly consider paying **$150–300 for a tax preparer** (or use **TurboTax Self-Employed** / **H&R Block Self-Employed** software) rather than DIY-ing the actual filing. After year one, once you've seen how it works, you can decide whether to DIY.

## Your entire ongoing system, summarized

| When | What you do |
|---|---|
| Once, now | Open separate business bank account + card, sign up for Wave/QuickBooks Self-Employed |
| Every time you get paid | Move 25-30% to a separate tax savings account |
| Monthly (10-15 min) | Glance at categorized transactions in the software, approve/fix any |
| Quarterly (Apr 15/Jun 15/Sep 15/Jan 15) | Pay estimated taxes via IRS Direct Pay using the software's estimate |
| Once a year (Jan-Apr) | Hand your bookkeeping export to a tax preparer or use TurboTax Self-Employed |

Do just these five habits and you will not get screwed — the biggest risk for new freelancers isn't complexity, it's not saving for taxes and getting surprised in April. The separate bank account + automatic 25-30% transfer fixes that by itself.
