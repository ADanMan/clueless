I just started freelancing as a designer (US, first year) — here's a simple system to keep you out of trouble.

## The 4 things that actually matter

**1. Separate bank account.** Open a free business checking account (or even just a second personal account) the moment you get your first client. Every payment you're paid comes in here. Every business expense goes out from here. Never mix personal and business money in one account — this alone prevents 90% of bookkeeping headaches.

**2. Save 25-30% of every payment, immediately.** As a freelancer, no one withholds taxes for you. The instant a payment lands, transfer 25-30% of it into a separate high-yield savings account labeled "Taxes — do not touch." This covers federal income tax + self-employment tax (Social Security/Medicare, ~15.3%). If you do nothing else, do this — it's the single thing that prevents a scary bill in April.

**3. Pay quarterly estimated taxes.** The IRS wants its money four times a year, not once. Deadlines are roughly April 15, June 15, September 15, and January 15. You pay a rough estimate using IRS Form 1040-ES (or just pay online at IRS Direct Pay — easier). Since this is your first year, don't stress about hitting the number perfectly — safe harbor rule: if you pay at least 90% of what you owe (or 100-110% of last year's tax, but you have no "last year" as a freelancer, so aim for 90% of this year), you avoid penalties. Rough rule of thumb: pay 25-30% of each quarter's freelance income.

**4. Track income and expenses in one simple spreadsheet (or app).** You don't need to "understand" bookkeeping — you need one running list:
- One tab: every payment received (date, client, amount).
- One tab: every business expense (date, what, amount, category — software, equipment, subscriptions, home office, etc.).
Keep every receipt by forwarding email receipts to a dedicated folder or using an app like **QuickBooks Self-Employed**, **Wave** (free), or even just **Google Sheets**. Wave is probably your best zero-cost starting point — it also lets you send invoices.

## Your setup checklist (do this once)

1. Open a separate business bank account.
2. Open a separate savings account, name it "Taxes," set auto-transfer of ~28% on every deposit if your bank allows it.
3. Sign up for Wave (or similar) — connect your business bank account, it auto-categorizes transactions.
4. Set 4 calendar reminders for quarterly estimated tax due dates (mid-April, mid-June, mid-September, mid-January).
5. Get an accountant or use a service like **Keeper** or **FlyFin** for your actual tax filing in year one — even just for the first year, it's worth the $200-400 to make sure you're doing it right and to learn what deductions apply to you (home office, software subscriptions, portion of internet, equipment, even a portion of your phone bill). After year one, you'll have a template to follow.

## Things to know exist (so they don't blindside you)
- You'll get a **1099-NEC** from any client who paid you $600+ in a year — but you owe tax on ALL income, even from clients who don't send one.
- **Self-employment tax** is the ~15.3% surprise most new freelancers don't know about — it's on top of regular income tax.
- Consider an **LLC** later for liability protection, but it's not necessary just to start — don't let anyone upsell you on this in year one.

That's it. Separate account, save ~28% automatically, pay quarterly, log everything in Wave, hire a cheap accountant for year-one filing. You genuinely don't need to understand the tax code to do this right — you just need the habits above running on autopilot.
